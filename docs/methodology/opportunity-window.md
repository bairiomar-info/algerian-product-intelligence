# Opportunity window

**Status:** M0 — Research and architecture. No opportunity score is defined.

## Concept

A product may have strong or accelerating demand while competition remains comparatively immature. The gap between demand trajectory and competition trajectory is a potential opportunity window. It is time-dependent, uncertain, and must be reported as evidence rather than a universal ranking.

## Separate trajectories

### Demand trajectory

Track level, velocity, acceleration, persistence, source diversity, and seasonality for a canonical product or concept.

### Competition trajectory

Track seller growth, advertiser growth, listing/offer growth, marketplace penetration, comparable-product density, and price pressure. Define market and denominator for each measure.

## Future measurable concepts

- demand acceleration;
- competition acceleration;
- seller and advertiser growth;
- marketplace penetration;
- price compression among comparable offers;
- time from first evidence to breakout;
- time from first evidence to saturation;
- persistence after an alert.

These dimensions remain independent. A report should show their time series, evidence coverage, contradictions, and uncertainty. It must not hide them behind an arbitrary “winner” score.

## Opportunity hypotheses

A candidate window may be described when demand growth is supported, competition maturity is lower or slower, persistence is adequate, and feasibility/local evidence do not contradict it. This is a reviewable hypothesis, not proof of commercial opportunity. A currently popular product is not automatically early or attractive.

## Validation

Define breakout and saturation events prospectively for a historical backtest, then measure alert precision, false positives, and lead time. Do not select thresholds on the final holdout.
