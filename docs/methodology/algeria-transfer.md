# Algeria-transfer methodology

**Status:** M0 — Research and architecture. This document is a placeholder until the corresponding research/design work is completed.

International popularity does not prove Algerian demand. Every transfer conclusion must retain its evidence, timestamps, source provenance, contradictions, and uncertainty.

## Evidence dimensions

- **Direct demand:** people explicitly searching for or discussing the product, brand, model, or close canonical alias in Algeria.
- **Adjacent demand:** people seeking related products, substitutes, features, or solutions.
- **Underlying demand:** evidence of the problem, job, or need the product addresses, independent of its international name.
- **Local competition:** Algerian sellers, listings, advertisers, offers, availability, and entry/exit activity.
- **Local price evidence:** observed Algerian prices with currency, unit/pack, seller, delivery, condition, and timestamp.
- **Local feasibility:** observable delivery, payment, supply, regulation, import, support, and availability constraints.

## Method

Build a bilingual/multilingual evidence map, resolve product aliases, collect permitted local observations, compare international and local price/offer contexts, and record positive, negative, contradictory, and missing evidence. Adjacent or underlying demand must not be relabeled as direct demand.

## Uncertainty

Represent provenance completeness, geographic coverage, source independence, entity-match confidence, freshness, sampling bias, price variance, and unresolved assumptions. Confidence is not probability until calibrated against outcomes. Unknown local evidence remains unknown.

## Output

An eventual report should separately state direct, adjacent, underlying, competition, price, availability, context, contradictions, and missingness. It should produce a qualified transfer hypothesis, not a binary claim based on international popularity.
